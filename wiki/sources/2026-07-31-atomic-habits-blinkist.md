## What This Source Contains

A Blinkist-format condensation of James Clear's *Atomic Habits*, structured as an introduction, seven chapters, and a final summary. The chapters build up a mechanistic model of habit formation (cue → craving → response → reward) and four corresponding intervention techniques: making cues obvious, making habits attractive, making them easy, and making them satisfying — plus tracking, accountability contracts, and habit stacking.

## Key Takeaways

1. Small, repeated behaviors compound into large results that are invisible in the short term; judge yourself by trajectory, not by immediate results.
2. Habits are automated behavior loops with four parts: cue, craving, response, reward.
3. Making a desired cue impossible to miss (environment design) and writing an implementation intention (a concrete when/where plan) both make new habits easier to start.
4. Motivation comes from dopamine released in *anticipation* of a reward, not only from the reward itself — a mechanism exploited by "temptation bundling" (pairing an unappealing habit with an enjoyable one).
5. Reducing friction (fewer steps, fewer decisions) and the "two-minute rule" (shrinking a habit to its smallest possible starting action) both make habits easier to adopt.
6. Because most valuable habits pay off in a delayed-return environment that our evolved brains aren't built for, attaching a small immediate reward helps sustain them.
7. Habit tracking (a visible record) and habit contracts (social stakes with another person) both increase follow-through.
8. Habit stacking — attaching a new habit to an already-automatic one — borrows the existing habit's momentum.
