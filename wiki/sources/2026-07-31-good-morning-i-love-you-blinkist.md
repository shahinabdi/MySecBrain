## What This Source Contains

A Blinkist-format condensation of Shauna Shapiro's *Good Morning, I Love You*, structured as an introduction, five chapters, and a final summary. It grounds mindfulness and self-compassion practice in neuroplasticity research and walks through concrete daily exercises: meditation, self-compassionate letter-writing, emotional-regulation technique, and habits that "prime the mind for joy."

## Key Takeaways

1. What you practice grows stronger — neuroplasticity research (including a Harvard study on meditators) shows repeated practice physically changes brain structure; internal practice, not external circumstance, moves baseline happiness.
2. The goal is liberation from limiting patterns, not self-improvement; setting very small goals ("five percent more") sustains motivation better than large ones.
3. Mindfulness has three elements — intention, attention, and attitude — practiced formally through daily meditation (even five minutes) and informally throughout the day.
4. Self-compassion means treating yourself as you would a close friend going through a hard time; it measurably triggers oxytocin and endorphins.
5. A structured self-compassion exercise for mistakes has three parts: notice the feeling and body sensation, name the experience as universal (common humanity), then offer yourself a kind statement.
6. Suppressing emotions makes things worse; the alternative is naming and welcoming them, remembering that emotions typically last no more than about 90 seconds.
7. Small repeated habits — smiling, a daily "three good things" gratitude exercise, and a morning question priming attention toward positive possibility — train the brain toward joy over time.
